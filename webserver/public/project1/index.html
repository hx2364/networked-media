Monday.

Alarm. The same one. You have to get to Brooklyn.


IMA meets at ten. The only way from here is the school bus.


Leave the house.

The stop.

Cold air. Same people, same coats. You wait.


When the yellow pulls up, the doors hiss open. You step in.


Get on.

Go back.

Inside.

Engine hum. Windows half fogged. Someone’s music through earbuds.


You watch the city pass. Same route every Monday. Same thought: Why do I keep doing this?


Get off in Brooklyn.

Start over.

Brooklyn.

You’re here. The building. The stairs. The hallway.


IMA is the one class where you don’t ask why. You just show up.


Walk into the room.

Start over.

IMA.

Screens on. People talking. Someone’s project on the wall.


You sit down. The commute fades. This is the part that sticks: not the bus, not the Monday, but the moment you remember why you’re making things.


Another Monday.
